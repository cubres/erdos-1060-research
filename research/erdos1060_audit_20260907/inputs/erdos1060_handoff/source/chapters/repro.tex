\chapter{Computational record and reproduction}\label{ch:repro}
\section{What was rerun for this handoff}
Fifteen supplied verifier programs were rerun successfully while compiling this
dossier. One additional large-range checker was interrupted by the enclosing
execution limit before returning a result. Its original certificate remains in
the archive, but it is not marked as newly reproduced here. The run log includes
script hashes, completion status and output logs. A successful verifier replays
its exact finite claims; it is not an independent proof of any infinite theorem.

The original archives are preserved byte-for-byte, and extracted working copies
are provided separately. Rerun outputs are stored with the handoff checks rather
than being presented as new discoveries. Some verifiers use Python assertions:
run ordinary Python, not \texttt{python -O}. Selected original discovery scripts
require third-party optimization or number-theory packages; the named exact
checkers use the standard library unless their README says otherwise.

\section{Reusable computational methods}
\paragraph{Exact inverse enumeration.}
Enumerate target primes and complete local exponent domains. Keep a residual
valuation vector. Reject a local block only when it exceeds a residual capacity,
a necessary row-support test fails, or even the maximum remaining contributions
cannot fill a required row. A completed leaf must exhaust every valuation.
These rules provide completeness; a resource-interrupted run does not.

\paragraph{Difference-column search.}
For one allowed base $p$ and a pair $a>b$, form
$c_{p,a,b}=\nu(h(p^a))-\nu(h(p^b))$, including every factor row. A collision
selects at most one signed column per base and has total sum zero. A row
supported only at one base excludes all nonzero columns at that row. Iterated
elimination is exact. Rational independence of all surviving columns also
certifies no collision. A floating-point MILP status alone does not.

\paragraph{Factor-row certificates.}
Output factors can be certified prime by recursive Lucas certificates. In some
larger computations, primality is unnecessary: checking that all row bases are
integers greater than one and pairwise coprime certifies their multiplicative
independence. Input bases must still be verified prime. Product/remainder trees
make the pairwise-coprimality check efficient.

\paragraph{Modular-root graph construction.}
For each target vertex prime $q$, generate the permitted residue classes from
multiplicative orders, then enumerate prime source values in those classes.
This reconstructs the complete bounded prime graph without factoring every
large divisor sum. The construction was separately compared with direct
local-divisibility tests on smaller complete prime ranges.

\section{Universal finite graph transversals}
For the universal cubefree graph on primes $3<p\le x$, the recorded certified
upper bounds on the number of vertices meeting longer positive cycles are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
x&10^3&10^4&10^5&10^6\\\hline
\text{vertices}&166&1227&9590&78496\\
\text{underlying edges}&396&3793&34536&314381\\
T_2(x)\text{ upper bound}&2&7&12&29.
\end{array}
\]
These are not certified minima. At $x=10^6$ one valid set is
\begin{align*}
 I=\{&17,31,41,43,53,71,73,89,97,101,127,131,139,151,157,\\
 &193,197,199,227,271,353,367,421,601,683,907,1117,2011,2467\}.
\end{align*}
After deletion only the mutual pair $\{13,61\}$ remains nontrivial. At $10^4$
the negative all-quadratic triangle $\{43,307,631\}$ also remains. Contracting
these explicit residual blocks gives an acyclic graph, which certifies that
there are no other cycles. The proof does not rely on a bounded cycle-length
search.

Fixing exponents at $2,3,I$ and splitting the exponent at $61$ into
$\{0,1\}$ and $\{2\}$ yields
\[
 \#\{k:h(k)=N,\ k\text{ cubefree},\ P^+(k)\le10^6\}
 \le18\cdot3^{29}\quad\text{for every }N.
\]
Unsigned all-cycle deletion sets were also checked at cap three with sizes
$15,43,110$ at cutoffs $10^3,10^4,10^5$, and at cap four with sizes $19,65$
at $10^3,10^4$. Those numbers include the small bases. They are finite heuristic
upper bounds certified by acyclicity, not asymptotic estimates.

\section{Complete target fibers and branch costs}
The deterministic exact solver gives the following completed records:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{target}&\text{cap}&\text{multiplicity}&\max\prod_i d_i\\\hline
M&2&2&2\\
M&\text{unrestricted}&2&2\\
336M&2&4&4\\
336M&\text{unrestricted}&6&9\\
N_7&\text{unrestricted}&7&8.
\end{array}
\]
Here $d_i$ are the actual branch arities along a successful path. Fixing
$v_{11}(k)=0$ or $2$ resolves $M$ completely. A selected 379-digit scaled
target also has two cubefree preimages and requires one binary branch; its
full factors are in the branch packet. Four smaller scaled cases and twelve
seeded random represented targets were also completed. These are selected
examples, not worst-case bounds.

Five dense represented targets were generated at prime cutoffs
$31,61,101,251,503$ by choosing exponent two when the entire quadratic divisor
sum stayed below the cutoff and exponent one otherwise. Their targets have
31,60,110,273,557 digits. Exact row propagation forces a unique cubefree input
in each case. Five polynomial-pair targets in Chapter~\ref{ch:bh} similarly
have unique cap-five preimages. These constructions stress the distinction
between apparent graph cycles and surviving integer choices.

\section{Archived inconclusive searches}
At several allowed-prime cutoffs, exact elimination left a residual model and
a subsequent optimizer found no witness before its time limit. Examples include
cubefree bases in $[101,10^5]$ (537 residual columns) and $[251,10^6]$
(1,412 residual columns on 602 bases). A further search beginning at 31 was
externally interrupted. No existence, nonexistence, or asymptotic claim follows
from these incomplete numerical searches. The residual columns are not labeled
actual exchanges. Earlier finite prime-cycle and collision witnesses remain
valid independently of an optimizer's status.
